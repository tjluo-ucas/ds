## Recommended tools

- Python 3, JupyterLab/Notebook, NumPy, Pandas, and SciPy;
- Matplotlib/Seaborn, scikit-learn, and statsmodels;
- PyTorch, Pyro, causal-learn, or instructor-approved equivalents when the task requires them;
- Git, locked environments, and automated tests; and
- a justification of assumptions and verification—not a tool merely because it is popular.

## Cloud notebooks

When local resources are insufficient and the instructor permits it, consult:

- the [Google Colab FAQ](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html) for runtimes, GPUs, and dynamic limits;
- [Kaggle Notebooks documentation](https://www.kaggle.com/docs/notebooks) for notebooks, accelerators, and sessions; and
- [Lightning AI billing](https://lightning.ai/docs/overview/faq/billing) for current credits, GPU consumption, and possible cost.

Resources change. Record Python/library/GPU versions, data hashes, and seeds. Never upload unapproved data, API keys, passwords, instructor solutions, or student personal information.

## Core reference directions

- Steven S. Skiena, *The Data Science Design Manual*;
- David Spiegelhalter, *The Art of Statistics*;
- Kevin P. Murphy, *Probabilistic Machine Learning: An Introduction*;
- Christopher M. Bishop, *Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning*;
- Hastie, Tibshirani, and Friedman, *The Elements of Statistical Learning*; and
- Josh Starmer, *The StatQuest Illustrated Guide to Machine Learning*.

Obtain books through the university library, publisher, or another licensed channel. Listing a title does not grant this site permission to distribute an electronic copy.

## Using AI assistants

Good uses include clarifying a concept, listing assumptions, inspecting code, proposing counterexamples, generating candidate tests, and explaining a failure. Do not ask AI to invent citations, data, or execution results, and do not submit work you cannot explain. Primary sources, actual execution, mathematical derivation, or instructor review still verifies every important claim.

## Asking for help

Include the goal, what you tried, a minimal reproducible input, the complete error, environment versions, and your current diagnosis. Never post student identity, grades, private data, or secrets in a public issue or chat.
