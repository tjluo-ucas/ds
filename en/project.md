## Objective

The capstone addresses a real scientific or engineering data problem, normally in a team of two or three. A model demo is insufficient. The project explains data generation and licensing, the problem hypothesis, baseline, evaluation protocol, errors and risks, deployment or use context, and the boundary of every conclusion.

## Three-stage delivery

| Stage | Time | Deliverable |
|---|---:|---|
| Proposal | Week 5 | Two pages: problem/motivation, related work, data size/features/license, ethics, success metric, roles |
| Progress | Week 8 | Three pages: EDA, baseline, frozen protocol, preliminary result, risk, learning curve, ablation |
| Final | Week 13 | 8–10 page report, 10-minute presentation, reproducible repository, data/model card, AI-use record |

## Rubric

| Dimension | Weight | Central question |
|---|---:|---|
| Problem and motivation | 15% | Who has which problem, why does it matter, and what is success? |
| Method and correctness | 25% | Is the method appropriate and are implementation, assumptions, and derivations correct? |
| Experimental design | 20% | Are baseline, split, metric, repetition, ablation, and statistics credible? |
| Results and discussion | 15% | Are errors, negative results, alternatives, limitations, and risks analyzed? |
| Presentation and reproduction | 25% | Can the repository be rebuilt, and are artifacts and individual contributions clear? |

## Required artifacts

- Problem and evidence statement;
- source, license, data card, and ethics/privacy statement;
- versioned code, notebooks, environment, and end-to-end run README;
- frozen split, seed, metric, baseline, and raw experimental results;
- model card, error analysis, calibration/OOD evidence, or scope boundary;
- contribution history, AI-use disclosure, and key decisions; and
- an honest account and next experiment when evidence does not support the hypothesis.

## Project gates

Any of the following blocks a pass: no clean reproduction; fabricated data, users, or experiments; exposed personal information or secrets; unidentifiable individual contribution; no credible baseline; train/test leakage; or conclusions beyond the evidence.

## Candidate directions

Scientific imaging and remote sensing, time-series forecasting, anomaly detection, causal inference, robust/OOD learning, human-AI decisions, AI evaluation, and tools for education or scientific discovery are suitable directions. Other proposals are welcome when data, users, ethics, and validation are feasible within the term.
